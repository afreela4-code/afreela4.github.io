How to Pick Your First Race

Finishable beats famous.

Updated September 8, 2026

Choose the Race That Lets You Finish

The best first ultra is not the one on everyone’s Instagram. It is the one that matches your training terrain, your life logistics, and a cutoff you can meet while walking the climbs. Use this with the readiness checklist and browse ideas on Races.

Decision checklist

  • Distance: First ultra for most people = 50K. Save 50 miles / 100K for later unless you already have a huge base.
  • Terrain match: Train on what you will race. Flat rail-trail ≠ technical mountain day.
  • Cutoff: Read it. If you are a back-of-pack runner, generous cutoffs matter more than scenery.
  • Aid & support: Frequent aid helps. Loop or out-and-back is easier for crew/family than a point-to-point shuttle circus.
  • Travel load: A local-ish race beats a expensive destination trip when you are still learning.
  • Weather honesty: Heat, altitude, and storms change everything. Pick conditions you can train for.
  • Vibe: Beginner-friendly races exist. Look for clear markings, welcoming culture, and realistic field size.

Busy-life filter: If the race requires Friday travel, complicated drop-bag logistics, and a 3 a.m. wake-up on top of a hard work week, it is still doable — but it costs recovery. Local and simple wins for race one.

Good first-race patterns

Red flags for race one

  • Huge elevation you cannot train
  • Sparse aid + long unsupported stretches
  • Aggressive cutoffs for your current long-run pace
  • “Sufferfest famous” branding with little beginner support

After you register

  1. Put the date on the family calendar (including travel).
  2. Point training at that course (see 16-week plan).
  3. Read the athlete guide twice — cutoffs, kit, drop bags.
  4. Plan race-week sleep and a boring, familiar menu.

FAQ

Should my first ultra be a trail race?

If you train on trails, yes. If you are a road runner, a gentler trail or rail-trail ultra is a kinder bridge than a mountain classic.

Is a timed loop (6/12/24 hr) a good first ultra?

It can be. You control how far you go, crew is easy, and you can stop when smart. Great confidence builder if you treat it as finish-focused, not ego miles.

What if friends are doing a harder race?

Let them. Your race one job is finishing and learning. You can stack harder events later with data from this one.
