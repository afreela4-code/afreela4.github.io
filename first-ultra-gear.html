First Ultra Gear Checklist

Buy what finishes. Skip the hype.

Updated September 8, 2026

What You Need vs What Can Wait

You do not need a sponsored kit for race one. You need shoes that fit, a way to carry fluid and fuel, a basic first-aid/blister kit, and a couple of layers that match the weather. This checklist is for a first 50K with a job and a family budget — pair it with Fueling 101 and the 16-week plan.

Newcomer rule: If you only buy five things, make them shoes, socks, hydration, practiced fuel, and a tiny first-aid/blister kit. Everything else can wait.

Must-haves (newcomers — start here)

If this is your first ultra, buy and train in this kit before anything fancy. Everything below should show up on long runs before race day.

  • Trail shoes that fit: Room in the toe box, half size up if your feet swell. Comfort on day one beats brand hype. Do not debut new shoes on race day.
  • Socks you trust: Blister-tested on long runs. Pack a spare pair in the vest if your feet get wet.
  • Hydration vest or reliable handhelds: For 50K, a vest with soft flasks usually wins — hands free, space for fuel. No bounce is the real test. Newcomers: see the vest guide.
  • Race fuel you have practiced: Gels/chews/real food already proven on long runs. See Fueling 101.
  • Weather-appropriate layers: Moisture-wicking base, packable shell if rain/wind is possible. No cotton.
  • Basic first-aid / blister kit (compulsory): Bandages, blister care (tape or blister bandages), antiseptic wipe, pain reliever you already tolerate, and any personal meds. Tiny bag in the vest — not optional for newcomers.
  • Headlamp if any darkness is possible (early start, delays, winter).
  • Phone + ID + a little cash/card in a zip pocket.

Nice-to-haves (buy used/clearance first)

  • Poles — helpful on big climbs; optional on gentler courses
  • Lightweight gloves / buff for cold starts
  • Extra body glide / anti-chafe beyond what’s in your first-aid kit
  • Watch with a simple timer for fueling alerts

Shop-smart rule: Last year’s model is usually fine. Clearance and prior-generation shoes/vests finish the same miles for less money. The trails do not check release dates.

Skip for race one

  • Carbon-plated “race only” shoes you have not trained in
  • Every gadget on the internet (multiple GPS backups, etc.)
  • Brand-new anything on race morning
  • Fashion layers that do not pack small or dry fast

Dress-rehearsal checklist (2–3 weeks out)

  • Full vest loaded like race day
  • Same shoes + socks + fuel
  • Long run on similar terrain
  • Note hot spots (rub, bounce, cold hands) and fix them

FAQ

Vest or handheld?

For a first 50K, most busy runners prefer a vest. Handhelds work on short/supported courses; they get annoying when you also need to eat and manage layers.

How many pairs of shoes do I need?

One solid pair is enough to start. A second pair helps if you rotate training volume — not required for race one.

What about a crew kit?

Nice if you have crew. For loop/out-and-back races you can often self-support with drop bags. Do not invent a complicated crew plan you never practiced.
