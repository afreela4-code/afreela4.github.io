Recovery & Staying Healthy

Consistency needs a body that can show up next week.

Updated September 8, 2026

Recovery is training

The plan only works if you can repeat it. Recovery is how busy runners stay in the game — after long runs and after race day. This is not medical advice; it is practical judgment for first-time ultrarunners.

After a long run or race

  • Eat and drink within the first hours — carbs + protein, familiar foods
  • Sleep like it is part of the workout
  • Easy walking the next day beats total couch lock for most people
  • Hold off on hard efforts until you feel like a human again
  • Note hotspots (blisters, tendon grumbles) while they are fresh

Training: niggle vs stop sign

  • Often OK to ease through: mild muscle soreness that warm-up softens, fading as you go
  • Back off: sharp pain, pain that worsens as you run, swelling, pain that changes your stride
  • Get checked: pain at rest/night, numbness, chest pain, or anything that scares you

Busy-life rule: Missing one run to protect the season is smart. Pushing through a sharp niggle to “stay perfect” on the spreadsheet is how DNFs and months off happen.

Prevention that actually fits life

  • 10% weekly load jumps max as a rough guardrail
  • Recovery weeks are mandatory, not optional
  • Sleep and stress count as training load
  • Strength 1×/week beats none
  • Rotate shoes if volume climbs

FAQ

How long to recover after a 50K?

Varies. Many people feel mostly normal in 1–3 weeks for easy running, longer before hard efforts. Let energy and mood lead, not the calendar alone.

Should I foam roll / ice / massage gun everything?

Use what helps you feel better. None of it replaces sleep, food, and smart load.
